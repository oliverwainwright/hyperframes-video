# What is SEO? — 30s script (5 scenes)

Scene timing: S = [0, 5.8, 11.8, 17.8, 23.8], 0.4s crossfades.

## Scene 1 (0–6.2s) — Hook
- **Headline:** What is SEO?
- **Explanation:** Search Engine Optimization: getting your page to show up when people search.
- **Visual:** A search bar draws in and types "best running shoes". Four result cards stagger in; three are grey skeletons, one is "yourshop.com" which lights up in indigo with a green "YOU" tag.

## Scene 2 (5.8–12.2s) — How search works
- **Headline:** Crawl. Index. Rank.
- **Explanation:** Bots crawl your site, Google stores it in its index, then ranks the best match for every search.
- **Visual:** Self-drawing vertical flowchart: bot icon (Crawl) → database icon (Index) → podium icon (Rank), arrows draw between nodes, the Rank node pulses green.

## Scene 3 (11.8–18.2s) — The three levers
- **Headline:** Three levers move you up.
- **Explanation:** Content that answers the search, a fast technical setup, and authority from other sites linking to you.
- **Visual:** Three cards (Content / Technical / Authority) with drawn icons stagger in. Below, a backlink diagram: five site nodes draw arrows into "your page", which glows as authority builds.

## Scene 4 (17.8–24.2s) — Climb to page one
- **Headline:** Climb to page one.
- **Explanation:** The top of page one gets most of the clicks. Next frontier: AEO & GEO, for AI answers.
- **Visual:** A ranking ladder of five slots above a dashed "PAGE 2" line. Your card starts below the line and climbs to the top slot while a position counter counts down 48 → 1 (tabular nums), then turns green.

## Scene 5 (23.8–30s) — CTA
- **Headline:** Get found where they search.
- **Sub-line:** Follow for more AI & tech explained in 30 seconds.
- **Visual:** Circular headshot scales + fades in with a rotating dashed ring, 12 seeded particles drift upward, a magnifier + up-arrow icon draws itself, tag "SEO, EXPLAINED IN 30s".

Facts note: no hard CTR percentages used — 2026 sources disagree widely (position-one CTR reported anywhere from ~20% to ~40%), so the video only claims the well-established qualitative point that the top results on page one take most clicks.
